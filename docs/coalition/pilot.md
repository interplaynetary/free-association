# **Piloting Free Association**

**Draft for Review v0.1** \[Nov 18, 2025\]

## **What a Pilot Involves**

A Free Association pilot demonstrates contribution-weighted resource allocation within a defined scope. Pilots begin with **X% of discretionary capacity** (e.g. 10-15%) and **established partner networks**, validating the approach while maintaining existing operations.

**Core Activities:** 

* **Publish** priorities/capacities/needs/contribution-recognition   
* **Derive** recommended allocation distributions from mutual recognition  
* **Review** and **allocate** resources using your existing systems  
* **Observe** **outcomes** and **adjust** contribution-recognition

**What Makes This Different:** The protocol generates allocation recommendations that inform your decisions — you retain full control over execution. Reduces application overhead, accelerates coordination, and maintains real-time visibility — **coordination through data rather than endless meetings**.

**Critical: The Protocol Informs, You Control**

The mutual recognition protocol generates recommended allocation distributions based on published priorities and recognition patterns. **These are recommendations that inform your decisions** — not automatic transfers or binding commitments. You review protocol outputs, apply your constraints (legal, political, operational), and execute allocations through your existing financial systems. You maintain complete sovereignty over every allocation decision.

**Relationship to Existing Frameworks:**

* **Article 9 Finance:** Transparent tracking of commitments and flows with automated reporting  
* **NDC Implementation:** Real-time capacity matchmaking for nationally determined priorities  
* **Loss & Damage Fund:** Rapid crisis response with pre-established recognition networks  
* **Global Goal on Adaptation:** Cross-stakeholder coordination infrastructure

**Governance & Sovereignty:**

* **Digital Public Good:** Open-source, no vendor lock-in, optionally self-hosted or white-label  
* **Data sovereignty:** Full control over your data, priorities, and recognition — participate via national infrastructure if required  
* **Exit rights:** Export data and recognition patterns anytime, return to traditional processes without disruption

## **Pilot Model Examples**

### 

### Model 1: Climate Finance Portfolio (Bilateral/Multilateral)

**Scope:** 10–15% of annual allocation | 15–25 existing partners | 6–12 months | Aligns with NDC tracking and Article 9 transparency requirements

**Published:** Priorities (adaptation, mitigation, technology transfer) · Recognition (100% to contributing entities) · Available capacity and timeline

**Derived:** Mutual recognition scores · Recommended allocation percentages · Real-time gap analysis · Co-financing opportunities

**Observed:** How recommendations align with actual allocations · Deployment speed vs. traditional processes · Transaction cost reduction · Partner satisfaction · Automated reporting for transparency frameworks

**Resources Required:** 2–3 staff weeks for setup · Open-source system (Extended AGPL License) · Optional: Technical support from coalition members

---

### 

### **Model 2: Loss & Damage / Emergency Response**

**Scope:** $500K–$2M response fund | 10–20 regional partners | Crisis-triggered | Pre-established recognition enables immediate coordination

**Published:** Available capacities (medical, shelter, logistics) · Recognition based on preparedness and past delivery · Real-time deployment readiness

**Derived:** Recommended crisis response allocations · Coordination patterns (who works with whom) · Real-time capacity availability across network

**Observed:** How quickly recommendations inform deployment decisions · Response time vs. traditional coordination · Duplication elimination · Resource efficiency · Coverage gaps identified in real-time · Performance tracking informs recognition adjustments

**Resources Required:** 1–2 staff weeks setup · Pre-crisis network establishment · Works alongside traditional coordination mechanisms

## **Pilot Model Examples (continued)**

### 

### **Model 3: Multi-Stakeholder Development Finance**

**Scope:** $5M–$20M allocation | 20–40 partners (governments, civil society, private sector) | Infrastructure and capacity-building | 9–18 months

**Published:** Country/sector priorities · Recognition across implementing entities · Multi-year commitment schedules

**Derived:** Recommended co-financing distributions · Gap identification (capacity without funding, funding without implementing capacity) · Complementary partner matching · Aggregate capacity visibility by region/sector

**Observed:** How recommendations inform coordination across funders · Commitment-to-deployment time vs. baseline · Alignment with national development plans · Complementary resource mobilization · Decision quality and partner satisfaction

**Resources Required:** 3–5 staff weeks setup · Optional MOU · Integrates with existing financial reporting systems

## **Implementation Timeline**

### 

### Weeks 1–4: Design & Mapping

Define scope, metrics, and recognition framework | Map existing partner relationships and contribution patterns | Identify technical/legal requirements | **Optional: Sign participation MOU**

### 

### Weeks 4–8: Technical Setup

Deploy system (open-source, self-hosted or coalition-supported) | Configure profile, priorities, and recognition network | Test allocation derivations | **Runs parallel to existing systems — no disruption to ongoing programs**

### 

### Weeks 8–12: Partner Onboarding

Introduce framework to partners | Support their network establishment | Coordinate capacity/need declarations | Align on data accuracy and adjustment protocols | **Partners maintain full autonomy — participation is voluntary**

### Weeks 12+: Live Operation

Review protocol recommendations and allocate using your existing systems | Compare protocol-informed decisions vs. traditional approach | Track efficiency, transaction costs, alignment, satisfaction, impact | Adjust recognition based on observed outcomes | **Protocol informs decisions; you maintain full execution control | Exit option: Return to traditional processes anytime with full data export**

### Months 6–12: Evaluation & Scaling

Compare to baseline metrics | Document lessons and present findings | Decide: continue with adjustments, scale allocation, extend scope, or conclude pilot | **Early adopter organizations available for peer consultation**

## **Support & Resources**

**Technical:** System deployment (self-hosted or coalition infrastructure) · Recognition framework development · Integration with existing reporting systems · Partner onboarding support · Ongoing troubleshooting

**Strategic:** Pilot design consultation · Metric selection · Change management · Internal stakeholder briefings · Peer connections with early adopters

**Legal/Administrative:** Participation MOU template (optional) · Data protection and sovereignty guidance · Audit and compliance documentation · Integration with procurement processes · No additional licensing or vendor agreements required

**Coalition Benefits:** Growing network across UN agencies, national governments, civil society · Shared learning and best practices · Interoperable systems · Collective visibility and policy influence

## **What Success Looks Like**

**Efficiency Gains:** 50–80% reduction in administrative overhead · 60–90% faster deployment · 15–30% increase in partner satisfaction · Maintained or improved impact

**Operational Shifts:** Partners report reduced fundraising burden · Real-time visibility into gaps and flows · Coordination through data rather than meetings · Recognition patterns reflect demonstrated contribution

**Network Effects:** Partners recognize each other, creating new collaboration pathways · Resources flow to highest-impact combinations · Demonstrates viability for scaling beyond pilot scope
