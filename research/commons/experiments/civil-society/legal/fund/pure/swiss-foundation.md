# Swiss Foundation

## Foundation Deed of Playnet Foundation

### Article 1  -  Name, Seat, and Form

The Foundation "Playnet" (the "Foundation") is established under Articles 80 et seq. of the Swiss Civil Code as an independent legal entity. Its seat is in Zurich, Switzerland.

### Article 2  -  Purpose

The Foundation exists to support and experiment with games/organizations that enable societal flourishing, particularly through:

* Supporting individuals and groups working on projects of public benefit
* Experimenting with computational coordination mechanisms
* Advancing knowledge about organizational design and collective action
* Funding projects that contribute to social, environmental, and cultural well-being

The Foundation pursues exclusively charitable and public benefit purposes.

### Article 3  -  Powers

In furtherance of its purpose, the Foundation may:

* Manage assets and reserves in any form, including digital assets
* Make grants and allocations to individuals and organizations
* Utilize computational, algorithmic, or automated processes for coordination and resource allocation
* Establish partnerships, collaborations, or participate in other legal entities
* Issue internal coordination mechanisms or units
* Delegate operational authority to designated persons, services, or systems
* Employ any other lawful means consistent with its charitable purpose

### Article 4  -  Participants and Beneficiaries

**4.1 Determination**\
Participants (potential beneficiaries) are determined computationally. A person qualifies as a participant when their Mutual Recognition Density (MRD) equals or exceeds the threshold value (currently 0.5).

**4.2 MRD Computation**\
MRD is computed weekly according to the Membership Module specification, available at [playnet.gitbook.io/docs/membership-module](https://playnet.gitbook.io/docs/membership-module).

**4.3 Participant List**\
The current participant list consists of all persons with MRD ≥ threshold as of the most recent computation.

**4.4 Status**\
Participant status ends automatically when MRD falls below threshold. Persons may also exit by written declaration.

**4.5 Legal Nature**\
Participants are not members in the legal sense and have no membership rights, voting rights, or claims on Foundation assets. Participant status indicates eligibility to be considered for Foundation support according to computational protocols.

### Article 5  -  Organ

The Foundation has one organ:

**5.1 Foundation Board (Stiftungsrat)**\
Comprises three persons with collective signature authority. Board positions are offered to participants in order of highest MRD scores. If a participant declines, the position is offered to the next highest MRD.

All Board members have equal authority. Roles exist only for legal requirements:

* One member designated as "President" (legal representative)
* All members are authorized signatories

Board composition updates when:

* MRD computation changes ranking, AND
* Current Board member declines to continue, OR
* Higher-ranked participant accepts position

### Article 6  -  Foundation Board

**6.1 Minimal Responsibilities**\
Board members have only these duties:

* Sign bank transfers as instructed by protocol
* Sign legal documents as required by Swiss law
* File required reports with supervisory authority
* Maintain bank account access
* Ensure compliance with foundation law

**6.2 What Board Does NOT Do**\
The Board does not:

* Decide who qualifies as participant (protocol computes MRD)
* Decide resource allocation (protocol computes allocations)
* Decide compliance filters (compliance service determines)
* Set strategy (emerges from participant activity)
* Make discretionary grant decisions

**6.3 Signatory Authority**\
Any two Board members sign collectively. All Board members have equal signing authority.

**6.4 Compensation**\
Board service is voluntary. Reasonable expenses may be reimbursed.

**6.5 Liability Protection**\
Board members execute protocol instructions mechanically. They are not personally liable for allocation decisions made by the protocol.

**6.6 Supervisory Compliance**\
The Board ensures compliance with supervisory authority requirements and Swiss foundation law.

### Article 7  -  Compliance Services

**7.1 Compliance Requirement**\
The Foundation must comply with Swiss AML (Anti-Money Laundering) and KYC (Know Your Customer) laws.

**7.2 Compliance Service Provider**\
The Foundation engages a compliance service provider to:

* Verify participant identities (KYC)
* Screen participants against sanctions lists (OFAC, UN, EU)
* Determine jurisdiction transfer limits
* Maintain compliance filter data
* Update filters when compliance status changes

**7.3 Compliance Filters**\
The compliance service determines Filter(Participant) for each participant:

* $0 = Cannot receive funds (sanctions, KYC failure)
* $X = Maximum allocation (jurisdiction limits, risk caps)
* Unlimited = No restrictions

**7.4 Protocol Integration**\
The protocol applies compliance filters computationally. The Board does not override or modify compliance filters.

**7.5 Compliance Service Selection**\
The Board may designate a compliance service provider. The service must be independent of the Board.

### Article 8  -  Resources

**8.1 Resource Allocation**\
Resources are allocated according to the Resource Allocation Protocol specification at [playnet.gitbook.io/docs/collective-recognition](https://playnet.gitbook.io/docs/collective-recognition).

**8.2 Needs and Allocations**\
Participants declare needs. External providers may declare capacities and allocate to needs. The Foundation automatically allocates its unrestricted funds to participant needs based on collective-recognition-shares and compliance filters. The Board executes approved transfers.

**8.3 Financial Records**\
All flows are recorded in a transparent ledger maintained per the protocol specification.

**8.4 Asset Management**\
Foundation assets are permanently dedicated to the Foundation's charitable purpose. All allocations must serve the Foundation's purpose as defined in Article 2.

### Article 9  -  Computational Protocols

**9.1 Protocol Specifications**\
The Foundation operates through three computational protocols:

* Membership Module (MRD computation for participant determination)
* Resource Allocation Protocol (provider capacities and need matching)
* Decider Process (parameter adjustments)

**9.2 Protocol References**\
Current protocol specifications are maintained at [playnet.gitbook.io/docs/decider/decider](https://playnet.gitbook.io/docs/decider/decider).

**9.3 Interface**\
The Board implements protocol outputs. Protocol computations are authoritative for participant determination and resource allocation.

**9.4 Purpose Alignment**\
All protocol operations must serve the Foundation's charitable purpose. The Board ensures allocations are consistent with Article 2.

### Article 10  -  Amendments

**10.1 Purpose Amendments**\
Amendments to Article 2 (Purpose) require approval by the supervisory authority and are subject to restrictions under Swiss foundation law.

**10.2 Protocol Amendments**\
Amendments to computational protocol parameters (thresholds, frequencies) may be made via Decider process among current participants, provided such amendments remain consistent with the Foundation's purpose.

**10.3 Structural Amendments**\
Other amendments to this Foundation Deed may be made via Decider process with weighted supermajority (66% support), subject to supervisory authority approval where required.

**10.4 Founder Reserved Rights**\

### Article 11  -  Dissolution

**11.1 Decision**\
Dissolution requires Decider process with supermajority (75% weighted support) and approval by the supervisory authority.

**11.2 Liquidation**\
Upon dissolution, assets must be transferred to one or more organizations with similar charitable purposes, preferably distributed pro-rata by collective-recognition-share (calculated across all participants as of dissolution decision) to qualifying charitable entities.

**11.3 Prohibition**\
Foundation assets may not be distributed to private individuals or for private benefit.

### Article 12  -  Supervisory Authority

**12.1 Authority**\
The Foundation is subject to supervision by the Foundation Supervisory Authority of the Canton of Zurich.

**12.2 Reporting**\
The Board submits annual reports and financial statements to the supervisory authority as required by law.

**12.3 Compliance**\
The Foundation operates in compliance with all requirements of the supervisory authority.

#### Board Selection Process (Opt-In Model)

**Position offers go by MRD ranking:**

```
Week 1: MRD computation ranks all participants
Week 2: Positions offered in order:
1. Highest MRD → Offered Board position
   - If accepts: Becomes Board member
   - If declines: Offer goes to next highest

2. 2nd highest MRD → Offered Board position
   - If accepts: Becomes Board member
   - If declines: Offer goes to next highest

3. Continue until 3 positions filled

Current Board members:
- Automatically offered to continue if still in top ranks
- Can decline to continue (position offered to next highest)
- Must explicitly accept to continue each term
```

**Term length:** Board positions reviewed quarterly after MRD computation.

**Example:**

```
MRD Ranking:
1. Alice (MRD: 1.8)
2. Bob (MRD: 1.5)
3. Charlie (MRD: 1.3)
4. Dave (MRD: 1.2)
5. Eve (MRD: 1.0)

Offers:
1. Alice offered → Accepts → Board Member 1
2. Bob offered → Declines (too busy)
3. Charlie offered → Accepts → Board Member 2
4. Dave offered → Accepts → Board Member 3

Final Board: {Alice, Charlie, Dave}
President designation: Alice (highest MRD of actual Board)
```

**No penalty for declining:** MRD score unaffected, full participation rights maintained.

#### Compliance Service

**Independent service provider performs:**

```
1. Participant onboarding:
   - KYC verification (identity documents)
   - Sanctions screening (OFAC, UN, EU lists)
   - Jurisdiction assessment
   - Risk evaluation

2. Ongoing monitoring:
   - Daily sanctions list updates
   - Quarterly re-verification
   - Status change notifications

3. Filter determination:
   For each participant, compliance service sets:
   - Filter = $0 (cannot receive: sanctions, KYC failed)
   - Filter = $X (cap: jurisdiction limits, risk levels)
   - Filter = Unlimited (no restrictions)

4. Protocol integration:
   - Provides filter data to protocol
   - Protocol applies filters computationally
   - Board receives filtered allocations
   - Board never modifies filters
```

**Service characteristics:**

* Independent of Board (no Board override)
* Automated sanctions screening
* API integration with protocol
* Transparent filter logic
* Auditable compliance trail

**Example compliance services:**

* ComplyAdvantage (sanctions/AML screening)
* Onfido (KYC verification)
* Sumsub (identity verification)
* Custom service built on these APIs

**Key separation:**

* Compliance service determines who can receive (filters)
* Protocol determines who should receive (recognition)
* Board executes transfers (mechanical only)
* No single entity has full discretion

#### Foundation-Specific: Purpose Alignment

**The Board's unique responsibility in foundation form:**

```
For each protocol-computed allocation, Board verifies:

✓ Does this allocation serve the charitable purpose (Article 2)?
✓ Is the recipient working on projects of public benefit?
✓ Does this advance societal flourishing?

If YES: Execute transfer mechanically
If NO: Cannot execute (would violate foundation purpose)

Example verification:
Protocol output: "Transfer $50K to Alice"
Board reviews: Alice's declared need is "water filtration systems in Uganda"
Assessment: ✓ Serves public benefit (health, environment)
            ✓ Within purpose (societal flourishing)
            ✓ Charitable project
Action: Execute transfer

Counter-example:
Protocol output: "Transfer $20K to Bob"
Board reviews: Bob's declared need is "Bitcoin speculation fund"
Assessment: ✗ Not public benefit (private speculation)
            ✗ Not charitable
Action: Cannot execute (violates Article 2)
Escalation: Refer to supervisory authority
```

**Key principle:** Board cannot change allocation amounts or recipients, but must verify purpose alignment. This is the one area where Board exercises judgment (required by foundation law).

#### Resource Flow: Protocol to Board Interface

**The distributed protocol determines allocations. The Board executes approved transfers that serve the charitable purpose.**

**Flow:**

```
1. Participants declare needs (visible to all)
2. Providers declare capacities (only providers, with actual resources)
3. For each provider capacity:
   - Protocol computes collective-recognition-shares within provider's set
   - Provider sees needs from participants in their set
   - Provider allocates based on recognition + needs + capacity
4. For Foundation's own funds:
   - Protocol detects available balance
   - Protocol automatically declares capacity: all current participants
   - Protocol computes collective-recognition-shares
   - Protocol applies compliance filters
   - Protocol allocates automatically
   - Board receives transfer instructions

5. Board verifies purpose alignment (foundation law requirement)
6. Board executes transfers that serve Article 2 purpose
```

**Example:**

```
Alice declares need: $50K for water infrastructure in underserved communities

Foundation has $100K available balance:
1. Protocol automatically generates capacity: all participants
2. Protocol computes collective-recognition-shares:
   - Alice: 28% (highest mutual recognition)
   - Bob: 22%
   - Charlie: 19%
   - Dave: 18%
   - Eve: 13%
3. Protocol applies compliance filters
4. Protocol reviews open needs
5. Protocol allocates:
   - Alice: $50K (water infrastructure)
   - Bob: $30K (regenerative agriculture education)
   - Charlie: $20K (community organizing)
6. Board receives transfer instructions
7. Board verifies purpose alignment:
   - Alice's project: ✓ Public benefit (health, environment)
   - Bob's project: ✓ Public benefit (education, sustainability)
   - Charlie's project: ✓ Public benefit (community development)
8. Board executes all three transfers
```

**Board's limited scope:**

```
Board CAN:
- Verify transfers serve charitable purpose
- Execute purpose-aligned transfers
- Verify providers have deposited funds
- Maintain accounting records
- Report to supervisory authority

Board CANNOT:
- Decide allocation amounts (protocol does)
- Change recipients (protocol does)
- Decide compliance filters (compliance service does)
- Override protocol for discretionary reasons
- Prioritize one need over another (except purpose alignment)
```

#### Foundation vs. Association: Key Differences

**Why choose Foundation over Association:**

```
Foundation advantages:
✓ More credible for large donors (permanent charitable structure)
✓ Tax benefits may be better (depending on jurisdiction)
✓ Asset protection (assets permanently dedicated)
✓ Clear charitable purpose
✓ Simpler structure (no General Assembly)

Foundation constraints:
✗ Purpose harder to change (supervisory approval needed)
✗ More regulatory supervision
✗ Higher initial endowment (CHF 50,000+)
✗ Assets cannot be returned to founders
✗ Board must verify purpose alignment (extra step)
```

**When to use Foundation:**

* Primary funding comes from large donors/foundations
* Want permanent charitable structure
* Purpose is clear and stable
* Comfortable with supervisory oversight
* Have initial endowment of CHF 50,000+

**When to use Association (Verein):**

* Want maximum flexibility
* Purpose may evolve
* Prefer lighter supervision
* Want General Assembly involvement
* Easier to launch (lower initial requirement)

#### Reconciling Distributed Decisions with Foundation Purpose

**Key insight:** Protocol allocates amounts and recipients, but Board ensures allocations serve charitable purpose.

**The flow:**

```
Layer 1 - Distributed (No centralization):
Participants declare needs (visible to all)
- Alice: "I need $50K for water infrastructure in Uganda"
- Bob: "I need $30K for agriculture education program"
- Charlie: "I need $20K for luxury vacation"

Layer 2 - Protocol Allocation (Computational):
Protocol computes collective-recognition-shares
Protocol allocates based on recognition + needs + filters:
- Alice: $50K
- Bob: $30K
- Charlie: $20K

Layer 3 - Board Verification (Purpose Alignment):
Board reviews each allocation:
- Alice's water project: ✓ Public benefit → Execute
- Bob's education program: ✓ Public benefit → Execute
- Charlie's vacation: ✗ Private benefit → Cannot execute

Layer 4 - Execution:
Board executes: $50K to Alice, $30K to Bob
Board reports to supervisory authority: $20K could not be allocated (purpose misalignment)
$20K remains in Foundation for future allocation
```

**No centralization of amounts or recipients.** Protocol determines those.

**Minimal purpose verification:** Board only checks charitable alignment, cannot change amounts.

**Board = Purpose-verifying execution robot.** Receives protocol instructions, verifies purpose alignment, executes mechanically.

Changing MRD threshold from 0.5 to 0.6

Decider runs → Result: Adopt 0.6 threshold (67% support)

```

```

All decisions follow same process. Board executes mechanically, seeks supervisory approval where required.

```
✓ Purpose remains charitable (Article 2 compliance)
✓ Assets used for stated purpose
✓ Proper financial management
✓ Board fulfills duties
✓ Annual reporting
✓ Amendments to foundation deed

✗ NOT: Day-to-day allocation decisions (protocol handles)
✗ NOT: Participant determination (computational)
✗ NOT: Operational details
```

**Annual reporting includes:**

* Financial statements (auto-generated from ledger)

* Compliance service reports

**Key principle:** Supervisory authority ensures charitable purpose served, but does not interfere with computational allocation mechanism (as long as allocations serve purpose).
